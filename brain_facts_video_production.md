Полный production-pack для ролика про человеческий мозг. Захватывающие факты + инфографика. Изображения — **Nano Banana Pro**. Видео/анимация — **Veo 3.1**. Формат — **16:9 (горизонтальный)**.

⚠ **Про формат 16:9**: это ландшафтный формат — идеально для **YouTube long-form / 16:9 каналов**. Для **TikTok / Shorts** нужен 9:16 (вертикальный). Если нужно для TikTok — скажи, перепишу. Сейчас всё под YouTube/16:9.

### ФАКТ 1 — «Электричество мозга» (Your brain runs on electricity)

**Хук-фраза для скрипта:**
```
[whispered, intimate] Right now, your brain is generating electricity. [pause] Enough to power a light bulb.
```

**Развёрнутая озвучка (~1300 символов):**
```
[whispered, intimate] Right now, inside your skull, a storm is raging. [pause] Not thunder. Not lightning you can see. But electricity.

[narrator, calm] Your brain contains roughly eighty-six billion neurons. And at this very moment, every single one of them is firing electrical impulses. Tiny voltage spikes racing from cell to cell at speeds that would put your home internet to shame.

[soft] The total electrical output of your brain is about twelve to twenty-five watts. [pause] That's enough to power a small LED light bulb. [dramatic] Your thoughts — your memories — your dreams — are literally made of electricity.

[narrator] Neurons communicate through electrochemical signals. When a neuron fires, sodium and potassium ions rush across its membrane, creating a voltage spike called an action potential. This spike travels down the axon at up to two hundred and sixty-eight miles per hour. [soft] Faster than a Formula One car.

[dramatic] And the incredible part? [pause] This electrical storm never stops. Not when you sleep. Not when you're in a coma. [whispered] The only time your brain goes fully silent is the moment you die.

[narrator, low] Right now, as you listen to this, billions of tiny lightning bolts are firing inside your head. [whispered] You are a biological power plant.
```

### ФАКТ 2 — «Мозг не чувствует боли» (Your brain can't feel pain)

**Хук-фраза для скрипта:**
```
[dramatic] Your brain processes every pain signal in your body. [pause] But it can't feel pain itself.
```

**Развёрнутая озвучка (~1300 символов):**
```
[dramatic] Here's something that should terrify you. [pause] Or maybe comfort you. Your brain — the organ that lets you feel a paper cut, a burn, a broken bone — has absolutely zero pain receptors.

[narrator, calm] Pain receptors are called nociceptors. They exist in your skin, your muscles, your joints, your organs. But in the brain tissue itself — nothing. [soft] Complete silence.

[narrator] This is why neurosurgeons can operate on a patient's brain while they are fully awake and talking. It's called an awake craniotomy. [pause] The surgeon cuts through the skull — that part hurts and needs anesthesia — but once they reach the brain itself, the patient feels nothing.

[soft] Surgeons actually need the patient to be conscious. They stimulate different areas with tiny electrodes and ask the patient to speak, move their hands, describe what they see. [whispered] If speech slurs or a hand goes numb — the surgeon knows they've found a critical zone.

[dramatic] Think about that. [pause] The one organ responsible for making you feel every sensation you've ever experienced — the agony of a stubbed toe, the pleasure of warm sunlight — is itself completely numb.

[narrator, low] Your brain feels everything. [whispered] Except itself.
```

### ФАКТ 3 — «86 миллиардов нейронов» (86 billion neurons)

**Хук-фраза для скрипта:**
```
[narrator, calm] You have eighty-six billion neurons. [soft] Each one connected to ten thousand others. [whispered] That's more connections than stars in the Milky Way.
```

**Развёрнутая озвучка (~1300 символов):**
```
[narrator, calm] Let's talk about the numbers. [pause] Because the numbers are insane.

[narrator] Your brain contains approximately eighty-six billion neurons. [soft] Eighty-six billion. That number is so large that if you counted one neuron per second, it would take you over two thousand seven hundred years to finish.

[dramatic] But that's not even the impressive part. Each neuron can form up to ten thousand synaptic connections with other neurons. [pause] That gives your brain an estimated one hundred trillion connections. [soft] One hundred trillion.

[narrator] The Milky Way galaxy — our entire galaxy — contains roughly one hundred to four hundred billion stars. [whispered] Your brain has more connections than that. By a factor of a thousand.

[narrator, calm] These connections aren't random. They're organized into networks — circuits that encode your personality, your skills, your memories, your fears. Every time you learn something new, new connections form. Every time you forget, connections weaken and die.

[dramatic] Your brain is not a static organ. [pause] It's a living, breathing network that rewires itself every single day.

[narrator, low] You are carrying more complexity inside your skull than exists in most of the observable universe. [whispered] And you use it to scroll social media.
```

### ФАКТ 4 — «Мозг жрёт 20% энергии» (Your brain devours 20 percent of your energy)

**Хук-фраза для скрипта:**
```
[narrator] Your brain is two percent of your body weight. [dramatic] But it devours twenty percent of everything you eat and breathe.
```

**Развёрнутая озвучка (~1300 символов):**
```
[narrator, calm] Your brain weighs about three pounds. Roughly two percent of your total body weight. [pause] But it consumes twenty percent of your total oxygen intake. Twenty percent of your calories. Twenty percent of your blood flow.

[soft] Pound for pound, your brain is the greediest organ in your body. [dramatic] And it never takes a break.

[narrator] Even when you sleep, your brain burns nearly as much energy as when you're awake. During deep sleep, it's busy consolidating memories, flushing out toxins through the glymphatic system, and maintaining critical body functions.

[narrator, low] This energy hunger has consequences. [pause] If blood flow to your brain stops for just four to six minutes, neurons begin to die. Irreversibly. [soft] That's why strokes are medical emergencies measured in seconds, not hours.

[dramatic] Your brain runs on glucose — sugar. When your blood sugar drops, the first thing you lose is cognitive function. You get foggy. Irritable. You can't focus. [whispered] Your brain is literally starving.

[narrator] This is why you crave sugar when you're exhausted. It's not weakness. [pause] It's your brain screaming for fuel.

[soft] Two percent of your body. Twenty percent of your energy. [whispered] The most expensive organ you own.
```

### ФАКТ 5 — «Скорость сигналов — 430 км/ч» (Signals travel at 268 mph)

**Хук-фраза для скрипта:**
```
[dramatic] The signals in your brain travel at two hundred and sixty-eight miles per hour. [soft] Faster than a Formula One car.
```

**Развёрнутая озвучка (~1300 символов):**
```
[narrator, calm] When you touch a hot stove, you pull your hand away before you even feel the pain. [pause] That reaction happens in milliseconds. And it's because your nervous system is absurdly fast.

[narrator] Neural signals — action potentials — travel through myelinated axons at speeds up to two hundred and sixty-eight miles per hour. That's four hundred and thirty-one kilometers per hour. [dramatic] Faster than a Formula One car at top speed.

[soft] Myelin is the secret. It's a fatty white sheath that wraps around your nerve fibers like insulation on a wire. It forces the electrical signal to jump between gaps called nodes of Ranvier — a process called saltatory conduction.

[narrator] Without myelin, signals would crawl at about two miles per hour. [pause] With it — two hundred and sixty-eight. [dramatic] That's a hundred-fold speed increase.

[narrator, low] But not all signals are this fast. Some travel slowly on purpose. Dull, aching pain uses slow unmyelinated C-fibers at just two miles per hour. [whispered] That's why you feel a sharp pain first, then a deep ache seconds later.

[dramatic] Your body runs two networks. [pause] A high-speed fiber-optic line for emergencies. And a slow dial-up connection for everything else.

[narrator, soft] And your brain manages both — simultaneously — without you ever noticing.
```

**Хук-фраза для скрипта:**

```

**Развёрнутая озвучка (~1300 символов):**
```
[narrator, calm] For decades, scientists compared the brain to a computer. [pause] They were wrong. Your brain is nothing like a computer. [dramatic] It's astronomically better at storing information.

[narrator] In twenty-sixteen, researchers at the Salk Institute discovered that each synapse in the brain can store approximately four point seven bits of information. Previous estimates said one or two bits. [soft] This single discovery multiplied our estimate of brain storage capacity by ten.

[narrator] The revised number: two point five petabytes. [pause] One petabyte is a million gigabytes. [dramatic] Two point five petabytes is enough to store three million hours of television. That's over three hundred years of continuous video.

[soft] But here's what makes it truly alien. Your brain doesn't store memories like files on a hard drive. [pause] A single memory is distributed across millions of neurons simultaneously. Fragments of sight, sound, smell, emotion — all stored in different regions, reconstructed on the fly every time you remember.

[narrator, low] This is why memories change. Every time you recall something, your brain literally rewrites it. [whispered] The memory you trust most might be the one that's changed the most.

[dramatic] Your brain has infinite shelf space. [pause] But nothing on those shelves stays the same.
```

### ФАКТ 7 — «Нейропластичность» (Your brain rewires itself every day)

**Хук-фраза для скрипта:**
```
[soft] Every thought you have physically changes the structure of your brain. [whispered] You are literally not the same person you were yesterday.
```

**Развёрнутая озвучка (~1300 символов):**
```
[narrator, calm] Until the nineteen-sixties, scientists believed the adult brain was fixed. [pause] Hardwired. Unchangeable. You were born with your neurons, and that was it.

[dramatic] They were catastrophically wrong.

[narrator] Your brain rewires itself every single day. Every new skill you learn, every habit you form, every trauma you experience — physically restructures the neural pathways in your brain. This is called neuroplasticity.

[soft] London taxi drivers who memorize the city's twenty-five thousand streets have measurably larger hippocampi — the brain region responsible for spatial memory. Musicians who practice for years develop thicker connections between their motor cortex and auditory cortex.

[narrator] But neuroplasticity isn't always your friend. [pause] Chronic stress physically shrinks the prefrontal cortex — the part of your brain responsible for decision-making and impulse control. [dramatic] Meanwhile, it enlarges the amygdala — your fear center.

[narrator, low] This means prolonged anxiety literally rewires your brain to be more anxious. [whispered] Your brain builds the prison and locks itself inside.

[soft] The good news? [pause] It works both ways. Meditation, exercise, learning new things — these physically rebuild the pathways stress destroyed.

[dramatic] Your brain is not who you are. [pause] It's who you're becoming.
```

**Хук-фраза для скрипта:**

**Развёрнутая озвучка (~1300 символов):**

[narrator, calm] On average, you dream four to six times per night during REM sleep cycles. Each dream lasts five to twenty minutes. That's up to two hours of vivid, immersive experience — every single night.

[narrator] But your hippocampus — the brain region that converts short-term memories into long-term ones — is largely inactive during REM sleep. [soft] Your brain is generating experiences it has no intention of saving.

[dramatic] Why? [pause] Scientists still don't fully know. One theory: dreams are your brain's way of processing emotions and consolidating important memories while discarding the noise. [soft] Another theory: dreams are random neural firing that your brain desperately tries to stitch into a coherent narrative.

[narrator, low] Some researchers believe dreams serve no purpose at all. [whispered] Your brain is just... hallucinating. Safely. Every night.

[dramatic] Think about that. Every human who has ever lived has spent roughly six years of their life dreaming. [pause] Six years of experiences — gone.

[whispered] What did you dream about last night? [pause] You'll never know.
```

**Хук-фраза для скрипта:**

[dramatic] But here's the strangest one. [pause] Everything you see, hear, and feel — already happened. [whispered] You are living eighty milliseconds in the past. Your brain is predicting the present.

[dramatic] But here's the strangest one. [pause] And once you understand it, you can never un-know it.

[narrator, calm] It takes roughly eighty milliseconds for sensory information to travel from your eyes, ears, and skin to your brain, get processed, and become conscious experience. [soft] Eighty milliseconds. That doesn't sound like much.

[narrator] But it means that everything you perceive — the words you're hearing right now, the screen you're looking at, the chair you're sitting in — already happened. Eighty milliseconds ago.

[dramatic] You have never experienced the present moment. [pause] Not once in your entire life.

[narrator, low] Your brain knows this. And it compensates. It runs predictive models — constantly generating expectations about what's going to happen next based on patterns it has learned. [soft] What you experience as "now" is actually your brain's best guess about what reality probably looks like — eighty milliseconds ahead of the data.

[whispered] You are not seeing the world. You are seeing your brain's prediction of the world.

[dramatic] And most of the time, the prediction is correct. But when it's wrong — that's when you get optical illusions, déjà vu, phantom sensations. [pause] Glitches in the prediction engine.

[narrator] Your entire conscious experience is a simulation. [whispered] Rendered eighty milliseconds too late. Corrected by guesswork. [pause] And you call it reality.
